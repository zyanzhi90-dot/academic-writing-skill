# D2 | Method-to-results drafting

Target: a generic robotics research journal. Draft connected English prose for the transition from stability-criterion methods to validation results. Use section/subsection headings if they help. Explain the model, both criteria, their relationship, and what the reported simulation and physical tests support. Equations may be used where supplied. Do not invent thresholds, numerical outcomes, proof guarantees, or tests. Do not reproduce source prose.

Author notes (P18):

- Platform: an underwater hexapod with six C-shaped legs and eight thrusters (four lateral, four vertical). It walks over rough terrain while affected by fluid forces, inertial loads, leg–terrain contact, cable forces, and actuator limits. A force-balance model combines body/leg support, thruster force and moment, contact friction, and motor torque limits.
- Modeling assumptions for the stated analysis: legs have negligible mass; the body center of mass lies at the center of the hip-joint plane; each leg's shape is treated as a rigid semicircle; leg–terrain friction coefficients are constant and identical; the six leg motors are identical. These idealizations limit any claim about real terrain.
- A vertical-thrust criterion computes allowable lower and upper vertical-thrust values under force balance, friction, and torque constraints. Call them `F_min` and `F_max`. The tested vertical component is admitted when it lies within `[F_min,F_max]`. The stability margin within that interval is `min(F_y−F_min, F_max−F_y)`. Too much vertical thrust can reach supporting-leg torque limits; too little can leave insufficient contact force against inertial/fluid loads, allowing slip. Vertical-thrust adjustment can increase the computed margin, but maximizing it can reduce walking flexibility.
- The vertical-only test ignores possible stabilizing choices of lateral thrust and moments. It can therefore reject a condition that a feasible full six-dimensional thrust vector could stabilize. The subsequent 6-D test uses feasible thruster forces and moments, including model relaxations and a robust variant with stricter friction constraints. It should not be described as identical to the vertical-only interval or as unconditionally necessary and sufficient on the real robot.
- Reported validation: simulation on a modeled rough-terrain environment evaluates changes in allowable regions and stability enhancement under varying loads/parameters. Physical climbing trials in a pool use a curved ramp and stairs; groups with different thrust settings compare the two criteria/enhancement approaches. The reported observations are compatible with adjusting thrusters and joint torques to maintain desired locomotion on those tested terrains. The supplied notes do not provide exact probabilities or universal robustness measurements.
