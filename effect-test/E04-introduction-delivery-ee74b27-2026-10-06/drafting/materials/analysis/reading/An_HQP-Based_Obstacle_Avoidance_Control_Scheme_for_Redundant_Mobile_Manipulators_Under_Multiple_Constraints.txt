An HQP-Based Obstacle Avoidance Control
Scheme for Redundant Mobile Manipulators
Under Multiple Constraints
Jingmou Nie , Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Yaonan Wang , Yang Mo ,
Zhiqiang Miao , Member, IEEE, Yiming Jiang , Hang Zhong , Member, IEEE, and Jie Lin
Abstract—It is a challenging problem for redundant
wheeled mobile manipulators to satisfy multiple physical
constraints and avoid obstacles while performing certain
tasks, not to mention constraining tracking performance.
In this article, a control scheme based on hierarchical
quadratic programming-prescribed performance control
(HQP-PPC) is proposed. The first layer of HQP can achieve
trajectory tracking, obstacle avoidance, and physical con-
straints. The second layer of HQP is used to guide the
direction of obstacle avoidance and produce a smoother
planned path. The prescribed performance function (PPF)
can ensure tracking performance so that the physical con-
straints and obstacle avoidance constraints are satisfied
in actual motion. Moreover, the proposed controller can
guarantee the tracking performance of the end effector due
to the characteristics of the PPF. Finally, the effectiveness
and superiority of the proposed method are verified by
simulations and experiments.
Index Terms—Hierarchical quadratic program, prescri-
bed performance control (PPC), redundant robot, wheeled
mobile manipulators.

I. INTRODUCTION
AWHEELED mobile manipulator robot (WMMR) can be
described as an n-degree of freedom (DOF) manipulator
mounted on a wheeled mobile robot. Since WWMRs have
a large working space and high DOF, they are widely used
in intelligent manufacturing [1], rescue [2], cleaning [3], and
nursing [4]. Much research has been done on the control meth-
ods of WMMRs in configuration space, such as model-based
control [5], adaptive control [6], [7], sliding-mode control [8],
and neural network control [9], [10]. However, most WMMR
tasks are usually performed by the end effector; therefore, the
study of control methods in the task space is more significant.
From the perspective of the dynamic level, the dynamic cou-
pling, nonlinearity, nonholonomic constraints, and uncertain-
ties between the platform and its mounted robot manipulator
make tracking performance difficult to guarantee. Therefore,
the controllers based on the dynamic model focus more on the
estimation and compensation of the unknown model. In [11],
Galicki proposed an integral controller in which the manipulator
coordinates and the task error plus the gradient of a kinematic
criterion function to be integrated to compensate for the er-
rors caused by the uncertainty of the model. In [12], a robust
impedance controller based on time-delay compensation for
uncertain mobile manipulators was proposed to suppress the
unknown disturbances. On the other hand, some researchers
have considered the redundancy of WMMRs and used the
null space and Moore–Penrose inverse of the Jacobian matrix
to solve the obstacle avoidance problem of mobile platforms
when WMMRs perform the main tasks [13], [14]. Furthermore,
Zhao et al. [15] exploited the redundancy of wheeled humanoid
dual-arm robots and achieved holonomic constraints by solv-
ing quadratic programming (QP). However, the aforementioned
controllers require additional methods to estimate the unknown
model or compensate for the disturbances, which makes the con-
troller too complex. In addition, these methods cannot guarantee
tracking accuracy and do not address the physical constraints
(position and velocity constraints) and obstacle avoidance prob-
lem simultaneously.
Determining solutions that better utilize the redundancy of
WMMRs has been the subject of numerous studies at the kine-
maticlevel.Theseincludeachievingrepeatablemovements[16],
[17], physical constraints [18] (joint position and velocity), and

obstacle avoidance [4]. To the best of the authors’ knowledge,
there are three main solutions, which are as follows.
1) Use the general weighted least-norm (GWLN) inverse
and null space to realize constraints [19].
2) Solve the QP problem to achieve motion planning [20].
3) Use reinforcement learning to obtain the optimal solution
for inverse kinematics [21].
In general weighted inverse kinematics, there are inevitably
singular problems [22]. The linear variational inequalities-based
primal-dual neural network (LVI-PDNN) can solve the QP
problem quickly [23], but it may not converge to an optimal
solution at some time points and cannot consider subsequent
movements,leadingtotrackingandobstacleavoidanceconflicts,
such as making the mobile platform stop for obstacle avoidance,
which may lead to failure of tracking. Reinforcement learning
takes considerable time, especially when the DOF of the robot
increases. Due to the updating of motion experiences, it is dif-
ficult for reinforcement learning to obtain sufficiently accurate
solutions through limited exploration [24]. On the other hand,
the aforementioned methods use velocity as the control input,
but they ignore the fact that the real-time velocity of the motor
is not necessarily the same as the control input velocity [22],
which may cause the robot to violate constraints on the actual
motion even if the planned path is optimal. Furthermore, since
velocity tracking is not considered, nondifferentiable functions
are used in the planner’s design process [4], [20], resulting in an
unsmooth planned reference trajectory and increasing the risk
of constraint violation in practice.
Overcoming the aforementioned drawbacks in a single con-
troller or planner is very difficult. To suppress and predeter-
mine the velocity tracking error, a dynamic model must be
considered because this is the only way to constrain this error.
Most existing online planning methods rely on accurate model
information [13]–[20], [22], [23]; however, the dynamic model
information is difficult to measure accurately, leading to unsatis-
factory planning results. Therefore, a planner-controller system
should be organized. The planner should be designed at the
kinematic level and must consider the velocity tracking error.
The controller must execute the planned reference trajectory
with high precision and determine the errors bound in advance.
HQP can realize subtasks as much as possible while executing
the main tasks [25]. This property of classifying tasks makes it
possible to better utilize the redundancy of robots and complete
multiple tasks with low computational cost [26]. Because of
this property, HQP is widely used in humanoid robots [27].
The aforementioned planning problems can be better solved by
adding subtasks that specify the direction of obstacle avoidance
and by considering tracking errors. For the control methods,
there are studies on high-precision control methods, such as
BLF-based control methods [28], [29] and error transforma-
tion [30]. The model-free prescribed performance control (PPC)
method has been widely used for robotic arms [31], mobile
robots [32], and multiple-input–multiple-output systems [33]
due to its insensitivity to model information and concise form.
However, this method has not been used to track the trajectory
of redundant mobile manipulator end effectors due to their
nonholonomic constraints and high degrees of freedom.
To summarize, an HQP-PPC scheme is proposed to avoid
obstacles without violating the physical constraints and track-
ing performance constraints. Compared to the aforementioned
methods [4], [22], the HQP-PPC scheme has the following
advantages.
1) HQP enables the subtask of providing obstacle avoidance
directions. In addition, the obstacle avoidance function is
optimized. These improvements can provide a smoother
reference trajectory, increase the probability of successful
obstacle avoidance, and simplify the design of a con-
troller.
2) By using prescribed performance function (PPF) to con-
strain the tracking performance, the bound of velocity
trackingerror canbepredeterminedandusedinthedesign
of HQP to ensure that the constraints are not violated in
actual motion.
3) In the HQP-PPC scheme, HQP fully exploits the redun-
dancy of mobile manipulators and provides a smooth
reference trajectory for PPC. PPC takes advantage of its
ability to set the bound of tracking error in advance to
ensure accurate tracking of the reference trajectory.
4) The proposed control scheme can ensure tracking per-
formance without violating the constraints and realize
obstacle avoidance.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Section II,
the WMR system is described. The controller design for WMR
is derived in Section III, including the introduction of PPF, path
planning, and control law design. Simulations and experiments
are designed in Sections IV and V, respectively. Section VI
discusses the defects of the proposed method and correspond-
ing solutions, and other possible methods for this problem are
probed. Finally, Section VII concludes this article.

III. CONTROLLER DESIGN
As shown in Fig. 1, The control objective is to design a
controller for the WMMR so that it can follow a desired tra-
jectoryOer = [Oerx; Oery; Oerz]withinaprescribedperformance
constraint while ensuring that the WMMR operates in safe areas,
such as achieving joint or velocity limits and obstacle avoidance.
The main ideas of this control scheme are as follows: HQP is
used to account for physical constraints and obstacle avoidance
to obtain reference velocity ˙
ξr. Control input u1 is provided
by PPC to ensure tracking accuracy. PPC provides the velocity
tracking error boundary for HQP and ensures that the constraints
are not violated even if there is a velocity tracking error. HQP
provides the reference velocity for PPC, which allows the robot
to avoid obstacles while tracking the desired trajectory Oer.

1) The scheme should provide a smooth reference trajectory.
In this article, to avoid producing an untraceable reference

2) The controller can ensure tracking accuracy and prede-
termine the tracking error.
The characteristics of the controller can provide the planner
with a known tracking error to obtain a reasonable trajectory and
ensure a better execution of the planned trajectory.
To make a controller with excellent characteristics for which
the tracking performance can be predetermined theoretically,
the BLF and PPF are used in [28] and [30]–[33]; however, when
approaching the boundary, these methods are at risk of chattering
and boundary breaching. These risks can be reduced in some
ways. On the one hand, increasing the control frequency as much
as possible can significantly reduce the robot’s motion in one
sampling time, thus decreasing the probability of the WMMR
approaching the boundary. On the other hand, reasonable control
parameters can also avoid risks to a certain extent. These control
parameters should be rectified several times in the experiment
to achieve the optimal control effect.
In addition to the methods mentioned and compared in this
article, there are other commonly used planning methods, such
as kinodynamic probabilistic motion planning. This planning
method uses heuristic algorithms on random discrete way points,
taking into account the dynamic constraints of the robot, to
reach the target position by node expansion and pruning of
erroneous nodes. In general, this method is usually applied to
point stabilization problems, and due to its predictive ability,
a sufficiently good reference trajectory can be provided in a
complex environment. In contrast, it requires higher computing
power.Althoughthisapproachisseldomappliedtothetrajectory
tracking problem, the idea of using the probability method to
produce an optimal trajectory may provide a new method for
redundant mobile manipulators.
VII. CONCLUSION
In this article, an HQP-PPF control scheme was proposed
to realize the end-effector tracking, obstacle avoidance, and
physical constraints of the WMMR. At the kinematic level, the
method used HQP to plan the movement in the configuration
space. The first-layer QP was used to avoid obstacles and realize
physical constraints, and a smoother obstacle avoidance function
was proposed. The second-layer QP was used to guide the
direction of obstacle avoidance. On the other hand, the PPF
can not only ensure the tracking accuracy of the end effector
but also accurately realize the planned trajectory so that the
actual movement of the WMMR can achieve obstacle avoid-
ance and cannot violate the constraints. Finally, the effective-
ness of the proposed method was verified by simulations and
experiments.
