This paper proposes an impedance learning method for rigid robot manipulators interacting with unknown, time-varying environments. The method combines an outer impedance learning loop with an inner adaptive position controller. A weighted interaction error cost specifies the requirements for trajectory tracking, tracking of the time integral of interaction force, or a trade-off between them. The outer loop combines gradient following and iterative improvement to adjust time-varying desired damping and stiffness according to this cost, while the desired inertia remains fixed at the apparent inertia. The learning update uses output errors and their rates of change without requiring identification of an internal environment model. The learning analysis represents the environment as a linear time-varying mass–damper–spring system. The learned impedance generates a virtual reference trajectory for the inner position controller. Adaptive compensation is added to proportional–derivative control to track this trajectory without requiring a robot dynamics regressor or the second derivative of the virtual reference trajectory. Under the assumed robot dynamics, bounded reference derivatives and suitable gain conditions, the inner controller ensures asymptotic tracking of the virtual reference trajectory and bounded closed-loop signals. Numerical simulation results for a PUMA560 manipulator and repeated wrist–hand interaction experiments on the Nancy robot demonstrate task-dependent impedance adaptation. Higher trajectory tracking weights lead to increased stiffness and smaller position errors, whereas lower weights lead to decreased stiffness and greater compliance at the cost of larger position errors. Simulations and the experiment with the higher tracking weight also show reductions in the interaction error cost.
