### Introduction

In human-guided contact tasks such as carving and polishing, an operator moves a robot while its tool interacts with an environment whose properties are unknown. The operator must be able to guide the motion with a manageable force, yet contact with the environment can disturb the intended path. The controller therefore has to address two coupled objectives: *manipulability*, or the ease of human guidance, and *contact stability*, which concerns the robot’s interaction with the environment and its trajectory tracking during contact.

Robot impedance affects both objectives. Increasing impedance can help the robot resist contact disturbances, but may require the operator to exert more force. Reducing that effort can, in turn, weaken performance in the contact task. This trade-off becomes especially relevant when the human and the environment act on the robot at the same time. Work on human–robot interaction has developed ways to accommodate guidance and respond to human motion or force, while work on robot–environment interaction has addressed contact forces and disturbances. Their different interaction targets leave a specific control question: how can a human-guided robot remain easy to move while adapting its response to an unknown contact environment?

This study uses information from human guidance to maintain manipulability and adapts robot impedance in response to environmental disturbance to support contact stability. The two objectives cannot be handled by simply adding separate controllers, because an impedance adjustment that serves one objective can oppose the other. For nonrepetitive tasks, the proposed adaptive impedance learning approach assumes constant environment coefficients and a constant environment stationary position. For repetitive tasks with spatially periodic disturbances, the approach is extended to an iterative version. Within the stated interaction model, the controllers are designed without prior knowledge of the disturbance or the human reference position. The manuscript examines the approach through stability analysis and H-MAN robot studies involving virtual disturbances, real carving, and repetitive polishing.

### Related Work

**Human guidance and manipulability.** Research on physical human–robot interaction has considered compliant responses to guidance, estimation of human intent or impedance, and adjustments to robot behavior based on human motion or force information. These approaches establish ways to make a robot responsive to its operator. The work described in the supplied notes primarily addresses the human–robot interaction; those notes do not establish how the same approaches handle simultaneous contact with an unknown environment. In the present task, human-guidance information must remain useful even as tool contact perturbs the robot’s motion.

**Environmental contact and disturbance response.** Research on robot–environment interaction has addressed contact-force control and disturbance compensation using feedback and learning methods. These approaches address demands arising at the tool–environment interface. The work described in the supplied notes does not explicitly include a person guiding the robot in the same interaction model. Human guidance introduces a coupled constraint: an impedance setting that resists environmental disturbance may also make the robot harder to move. The present study addresses that coupling by considering guidance and contact within one control problem, with separate adaptations for nonrepetitive and spatially repetitive task conditions.
