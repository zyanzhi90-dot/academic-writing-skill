# Paper type: research

The reader of a research paper asks:

- why the phenomenon matters
- what was done
- what was found
- what it means

Apply these questions within the current request and the author's scientific
content. Preserve author facts, technical conditions, terminology, and supported
claim strength; reader questions do not justify inventing scientific content.

## Hourglass structure

An hourglass is one useful structure when it fits the scientific task:

- `Introduction`: open broadly, then narrow to the specific gap, question, hypothesis, methods, and study
- `Discussion/Conclusion`: widen again, connecting the findings back to the literature and explaining how the knowledge gap was filled

Preserve reasonable alternative structures and accurate natural expressions.
Reorganize only when a concrete problem obscures the current part's purpose,
scientific relations, or evidence; retain necessary qualifiers and respect the
requested edit scope. If author intent or essential evidence is unresolved,
place the issue in revision notes outside prose rather than inventing a link.

## Productive writing order

Evidence-first reasoning helps diagnose claims and support; it does not require
editing sections in a fixed sequence. Polish or revise the specified section,
paragraph group, or feedback target directly when its scientific content is
clear, using the loaded core diagnosis and expression checks.

A working abstract can be revised before the body is complete from supported
author material; align the final abstract with actual results, guarantees, and
boundaries. Keep its working status and unresolved input questions outside
manuscript prose. Do not add findings to fit a generic abstract or paper pattern.
