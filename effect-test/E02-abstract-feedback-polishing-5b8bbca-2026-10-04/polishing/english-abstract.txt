This paper proposes an impedance learning method for rigid manipulators interacting with unknown, time-varying environments. The method adjusts time-varying desired damping and stiffness through repeated interactions to meet task requirements for trajectory-tracking accuracy and compliance. Gradient following and iterative improvement are combined to update the impedance parameters from interaction feedback according to a weighted interaction error cost, without precisely identifying the parameters of the linear time-varying mass–damper–spring environment. An adaptive position controller is designed to track the virtual reference trajectory generated by the desired impedance and thus realize the learned impedance without requiring a regressor matrix for the robot dynamics. For the considered manipulator dynamics, theoretical analysis establishes asymptotic tracking of the virtual reference trajectory and bounded closed-loop signals, provided that the virtual-reference velocity and acceleration are bounded, the control gains are appropriately selected and the adaptive parameter matrices are positive definite. Numerical simulation results demonstrate reductions in the interaction error cost under two trajectory-tracking weight settings. Experiments with repeated wrist–hand contact further demonstrate task-dependent adjustment of the trade-off between trajectory-tracking accuracy and compliance.
