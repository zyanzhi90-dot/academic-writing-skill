Fixed-Time Neural Control of Robot Manipulator
With Global Stability and Guaranteed
Transient Performance
Chengzhi Zhu, Yiming Jiang , and Chenguang Yang , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Nowadays, due to many limitations in reality,
the optimization of tracking precision and convergence
time has attracted the attention of researchers in robotics
community. In this article, a fixed-time adaptive neural net-
work (NN) controller is proposed for unknown robot ma-
nipulators. A switching mechanism is integrated into the
control design such that the semiglobal stability of the
conventional NN control systems can be extended to global
stability. The time-varying barrier Lyapunov function and
the fixed-time control technique are incorporated into the
controller design to guarantee the prescribed motion con-
straints and the fixed-time convergence simultaneously.
Compared with some existing fixed-time NN control algo-
rithms, the assumption that NN weight should be upper
bounded can be relaxed in our work. Finally, the simulation
and experiment studies are respectively carried out based
on an unknown 2-degree of freedom robot and a Baxter
robot to demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of
the proposed control scheme.
Index Terms—Barrier Lyapunov functions (BLFs), fixed-
time convergence, global stability, neural network (NN) con-
trol, robot manipulator.

Most of the proposed control schemes focused on the steady-
state tracking accuracy, but few of them involved the transient
performance and convergence time.
In practice, the undesirable transient performance may lead to
the collision between robots and the surrounding environment,
thus causing the system safety problems. Therefore, different
controllers have been proposed for robots in the presence of vari-
ous constraints to achieve desired transient performance [4]–[6].
Recently, the barrier Lyapunov functions (BLFs) were originally
proposed to indirectly achieve the state and output constraints
in the nonlinear control community [7], [8]. In [7], an adaptive
observer-based fuzzy control scheme using BLF has been pro-
posed for a class of systems. In [8], a class of tracking control
problems has been investigated for a class of nonlinear systems
with deferred full-state constraints.
Due to the complexity of the robot mechanism, the model
information about the robots is not available in most practical
cases.Tosolvethisproblem,thedesignedcontrollershouldcom-
pensate the system uncertainty by the input–output data. Neural
network (NN) and fuzzy logic technique have been widely
employed in intelligent controller design for the unknown sys-
tems [6], [9], [10]. However, the above-mentioned NN control
schemes can only guarantee semiglobally uniformly ultimately
boundedness (SGUUB) of the involved systems, for which the
approximation ability of the NN has been greatly attenuated.
So it is significant to extend the stability of the NN control
schemes from semiglobal stability to global stability. In [11],
an adaptive NN controller has been proposed for hypersonic
flight vehicle systems to achieve global uniformly ultimately
boundedness (GUUB).
Some existing control schemes have been proposed and fo-
cused on the convergence time of the systems. However, for
the proposed finite-time control algorithms [12], [13], the con-
vergence time is always related to the initial conditions, which
are always unavailable in practice. Recently, various fixed-time
control algorithms have caught the attention of researchers and
been employed in the nonlinear control community [14]–[16].
In [15], the distributed fixed-time algorithm has been proposed
for unknown multiple Euler–Lagrange systems under directed
graphs. In [16], a fixed-time fuzzy control scheme has been pro-
posed for the tracking problem of a class of nonlinear systems.
So far, there are few researches that combine NN technique
and fixed-time control algorithm [17]–[23]. Motivated by [23],
a global fixed-time NN controller is proposed for unknown robot

A BLF has been employed in the controller design such that the

future work. The main contributions of our work are listed as
follows.
1) A proper BLF has been integrated into the NN controller
design such that the prescribed transient performance can
be guaranteed and the tracking errors will not violate the

2) The tracking performance of the controlled robot system

tial conditions.
3) A switching mechanism has been combined with the

signals achieve GUUB such that the assumption that the

The objective of the controller design is to make the joint
position q in (1) track the desired trajectory qd with fixed-time
convergence regardless of the initial conditions. Meanwhile, the
output constraints should not be violated during the control

VI. CONCLUSION
In this article, a global fixed-time NN controller was pro-
posed for unknown robot manipulators with predefined output
constraints. A switching mechanism was integrated into the
controller design such that SGUUB in the traditional NN control
schemes can be extended to GUUB. The system Lyapunov
function satisfies two different forms of inequalities like (45)
and (50) simultaneously such that the assumption that the NN
weight estimation is bounded can be relaxed compared with the
existing fixed-time NN control schemes [17]–[19]. A BLF was
exploited such that the tracking errors can be constrained in the
predefined range, rather than the uncertain convergence domain
in most practical fixed-time algorithms [17], [18], [27]. On the
whole, compared with the traditional NN control algorithms, the
proposed NN controller in this article can guarantee the global
stability and the fixed-time convergence simultaneously without
violating the predefined output constraints, which showed the
superiority of the control scheme.
In the future work, the neural control problem of unknown
bimanual robots with fixed-time convergence will be discussed.
